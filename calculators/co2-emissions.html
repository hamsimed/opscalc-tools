    Electricity CO₂ Emissions Calculator

    Scope 2 emissions from purchased electricity equal the energy consumed multiplied by an emission factor. The factor depends on the country, the year and the reporting method, so this calculator asks for it instead of assuming one.

    Your inputs

    kWh

    kg CO₂e/kWh

    Use the factor published by your utility or national authority for the reporting year.

    t (or units)

    Formula

    Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Electricity (kWh) × Emission factor (kg CO₂e/kWh)Intensity = Emissions ÷ Production output
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    Emission factorGrid factor for the location and year (location-based method)kg CO₂e/kWh
    OutputProduction in the same period and boundaryt or units

    Worked example

    A conveyor drive used 306,383 kWh in a year. The illustrative grid factor is 0.45 kg CO₂e/kWh (replace it with your official factor), and the system moved 920,000 tonnes.

    Inputs
    Electricity consumed306,383 kWh
    Grid emission factor0.45 kg CO₂e/kWh
    Production output (optional)920,000 t (or units)
    Results
    Emissions (Scope 2, location-based)137.87 t CO₂e
    Emissions137,872 kg CO₂e
    Emission intensity0.1499 kg CO₂e per t/unit

    The result is only as good as the emission factor. State the factor, its source and year whenever you report the figure.

    Choosing the emission factor

    Use the factor published by your national authority, grid operator or utility for the reporting year. The GHG Protocol distinguishes the location-based method (average grid factor) from the market-based method (contractual instruments such as green tariffs). Report which method and factor you used. The example value on this page is illustrative only.

    Linking energy and emissions

    Every kWh saved with the motor efficiency, VSD or compressed air calculators can be converted here into avoided emissions.

    Common mistakes

    • Using a factor from another country or year.
    • Mixing location-based and market-based figures in one report.
    • Dividing by output from a different boundary than the energy meter.

    Where it’s used

    • Scope 2 reporting for a site or line.
    • Expressing energy projects in avoided CO₂e.
    • Emission intensity indicators per tonne produced.

    Questions

    Does this include Scope 1 emissions?

    No. Fuels burned on site (boilers, vehicles) are Scope 1 and use fuel-specific factors.
