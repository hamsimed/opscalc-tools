    Inventory Carrying Cost Calculator

    Inventory costs money every day it sits on the shelf: capital tied up, warehouse space, insurance and the risk of damage or obsolescence. This calculator turns those components into an annual carrying cost and a single carrying cost rate.

    Formula

    Carrying cost rate = Capital % + Storage % + Insurance % + Risk %Annual carrying cost = Average inventory value × Carrying cost rate
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    Average inventory valueInventory at cost, averaged over the yearcurrency
    RatesAnnual cost of each component as a share of inventory value% per year

    Worked example

    A plant holds 400,000 of inventory on average. Capital costs 10% a year, storage and handling 6%, insurance 2% and obsolescence and shrinkage 5%.

    Use the carrying cost rate as the holding cost input in the EOQ calculator: holding cost per unit = unit cost × rate.

    Where the rates come from

    • Capital: your cost of capital or the return you could earn elsewhere.
    • Storage and handling: warehouse cost (rent or depreciation, energy, staff) divided by average inventory value.
    • Risk: write-offs, damage and shrinkage from the last few years, divided by average inventory.

    The rate feeds the EOQ calculator: holding cost per unit = unit cost × rate.

    Common mistakes

    • Counting storage costs that would not change if inventory fell.
    • Using an optimistic obsolescence rate for spares or seasonal goods.
    • Applying one rate to items with very different risk profiles.

    Where it’s used

    • Inventory reduction business cases.
    • EOQ and safety stock settings.
    • Comparing in-house stock with consignment or vendor-managed inventory.

    Questions

    Is the carrying cost rate the same as the interest rate?

    No. Interest or capital cost is only one component; storage, insurance and risk are added to it.
