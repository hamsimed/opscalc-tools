DPMO & Sigma Level Calculator

Defects per million opportunities (DPMO) normalises defect counts so that simple and complex products can be compared. The calculator converts DPMO into process yield and the Six Sigma "sigma level", using the conventional 1.5-sigma shift.

Defining opportunities carefully

DPMO is very sensitive to how opportunities are counted. Adding trivial "opportunities" lowers DPMO without any real improvement. Count only characteristics that are critical to the customer and actually checked. Then keep the definition fixed over time.

About the 1.5σ shift

Six Sigma tables add 1.5 to the z-value to allow for long-term process drift. That is why 3.4 DPMO is called "six sigma". The shift is a convention, not a measured property of your process. If you quote a sigma level, say whether it includes the shift.

Frequently asked questions

What DPMO corresponds to six sigma?

By convention, 3.4 DPMO is six sigma with the 1.5σ shift included. Without the shift, 3.4 DPMO corresponds to about 4.5σ.

Why does the calculator not show sigma when there are no defects?

With zero observed defects, the sample cannot estimate a sigma level. You can only say the true defect rate is probably below a bound that depends on sample size.
