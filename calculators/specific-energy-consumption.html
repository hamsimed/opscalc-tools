    Specific Energy Consumption Calculator

    Specific energy consumption (SEC) is energy used per unit of output, such as kWh per tonne. It is one of the most widely used energy performance indicators (EnPIs). Compare a period with a baseline to see whether energy performance has improved and by how much.

    Formula

    SEC = Energy consumed ÷ Production outputChange vs. baseline = SEC ÷ Baseline SEC − 1Energy saved = (Baseline SEC − SEC) × Production output

    A simple ratio baseline ignores fixed (load-independent) consumption. If output varied a lot, a regression baseline (E = a·output + b) is more reliable.

    When a simple ratio misleads

    Most processes have a fixed energy component (lighting, auxiliaries, idling equipment) that does not change with output. When output drops, SEC rises even if nothing got worse. ISO 50006 recommends checking whether a ratio EnPI is suitable or whether a regression model (energy = a × output + b) better represents the relationship. With a model, savings are calculated against the expected energy for the actual output.

    Good practice

    • Keep the energy and output boundaries identical.
    • Record relevant variables (product mix, ore hardness, ambient temperature) alongside SEC.
    • Track SEC at the level where someone can act on it: a line or a major energy user, not just the whole site.

    Common mistakes

    • Different boundaries for energy and output.
    • Reading a rise in SEC at low output as worse performance.
    • Comparing sites with different product mixes.

    Where it’s used

    • Energy performance indicators (EnPIs) for an ISO 50001 energy management system.
    • Monthly energy reviews.
    • Benchmarking lines or periods with similar mix.

    Questions

    Is SEC the same as energy intensity?

    Broadly yes. Energy intensity is often used for economic output (energy per unit of revenue), while SEC usually refers to physical output.
