    NPV & IRR Calculator

    Net present value converts future cash flows into today’s money using a discount rate, then subtracts the investment. A positive NPV means the project earns more than the required return. This calculator handles the common case of equal annual cash flows over the project life, with an optional salvage value.

    Worked example

    A variable-speed drive retrofit costs 85,000, saves 31,000 a year for 8 years and has a residual value of 5,000. The company uses a 10% discount rate.

    Inputs
    Initial investment85,000
    Annual net cash inflow31,000 per year
    Project life8 years
    Discount rate10 % per year
    Salvage value at end of life (optional)5,000
    Results
    Net present value (NPV)82,715
    Internal rate of return (IRR)32.93%
    Profitability index1.97
    Discounted payback3.37 years

    NPV is positive: at this discount rate the project creates value. Cash flows are assumed equal each year and received at year end.

    NPV, IRR and payback together

    Payback tells you how fast money returns, IRR the return rate, NPV the value created. They can disagree: a short project with a high IRR may create less value than a longer one with a lower IRR. For mutually exclusive choices, rank by NPV at the company’s discount rate. Use the payback calculator as a first screen.

    Choosing the discount rate

    Use your organisation’s hurdle rate or weighted average cost of capital, adjusted for project risk. Your finance team normally sets it.

    Common mistakes

    • Discounting savings that are not cash (allocated overheads that do not change).
    • Using a discount rate that ignores project risk.
    • Comparing IRRs of projects of very different size or duration.

    Where it’s used

    • Capital requests for maintenance and energy projects.
    • Comparing repair-versus-replace options.
    • Lease, buy or retrofit decisions.

    Questions

    What if cash flows differ each year?

    This version assumes equal annual flows. For uneven flows, discount each year separately in a spreadsheet; the method is the same.
