    Electrical Power Calculator (kW, kVA, kvar)

    Convert measured voltage, current and power factor into active, apparent and reactive power for three-phase or single-phase loads. Add motor efficiency to estimate shaft output. This is useful for checking a motor’s real load against its nameplate.

    Formula

    Three-phase apparent power S = √3 × V × ISingle-phase apparent power S = V × IActive power P = S × PFReactive power Q = √(S² − P²)

    Worked example

    A clamp meter reads 135 A on a 400 V three-phase motor supply, with a power factor of 0.86. The motor efficiency is 94.5%.

    Inputs
    SupplyThree-phase (line-to-line voltage)
    Voltage400 V
    Current135 A
    Power factor0.86 cos φ
    Efficiency (optional)94.5 %
    Results
    Active power (input)80.44 kW
    Apparent power93.53 kVA
    Reactive power47.73 kvar
    Estimated shaft output76.01 kW

    Practical notes

    • Measure current on all three phases and use the average. Unbalance above a few percent is a problem in its own right.
    • With variable-speed drives, harmonics make the true power factor lower than cos φ. A power analyser gives more reliable kW.
    • Comparing estimated shaft output with nameplate kW shows the motor’s load. A motor that runs well below rating most of the time may be oversized.

    Estimating motor load from current

    Measured kW divided by the motor’s rated input kW (rated output ÷ rated efficiency) gives a load estimate. For example, if the motor measured above is rated 90 kW with 95% efficiency, its rated input is about 94.7 kW, so 80.4 kW measured means about 85% load. Current alone is a poor load indicator at light load, because the magnetising current stays almost constant while power factor drops.

    Common mistakes

    • Using phase voltage in the three-phase formula.
    • Estimating load from current alone at light load.
    • Ignoring harmonics with variable-speed drives.

    Where it’s used

    • Checking motor load from clamp-meter readings.
    • Sizing cables and protection.
    • Preparing power-factor studies.

    Questions

    Why multiply by √3 for three-phase?

    With line-to-line voltage V and line current I in a balanced three-phase system, total apparent power is √3 × V × I. It is the sum of the three phase powers expressed in line quantities.
