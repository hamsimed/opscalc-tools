    Formula

    Electrical input (kW) = Rated power × Load ÷ EfficiencyEnergy per day (kWh) = Electrical input × Hours per dayAnnual cost = Energy per day × Operating days × Price per kWh
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    Rated powerNameplate power of the loadkW
    Average loadAverage share of rated power actually drawn%
    EfficiencyMotor efficiency at that load; leave at 100% for non-motor loads with input rating%
    Energy priceAverage price per kWh in your currencyper kWh

    Worked example

    A 75 kW conveyor drive motor runs at about 80% load, 16 hours a day, 300 days a year. Efficiency is 94% and energy costs 0.12 per kWh.

    Inputs
    Rated power75 kW
    Average load80 % of rated
    Motor efficiency (optional)94 %
    Operating hours per day16 h
    Operating days per year300 days
    Energy price0.12 per kWh
    Results
    Annual energy cost36,766
    Annual energy use306,383 kWh
    Average monthly cost3,064
    Cost per operating day122.55
    Cost per operating hour7.66
    Electrical input power63.83 kW

    Energy-only estimate: excludes demand (kW) charges, reactive-energy penalties, taxes and time-of-use tiers.

    What this estimate leaves out

    • Demand charges: many industrial tariffs bill peak kW as well as kWh.
    • Reactive energy: low power factor can trigger penalties. See the power factor correction calculator.
    • Time-of-use rates: shifting load to off-peak hours changes the average price.

    For a budget-grade figure, use the average price from recent bills (total cost ÷ total kWh).

    Getting a better load figure

    Nameplate power overstates consumption for most motors, which seldom run at full load. A clamp meter reading combined with the electrical power calculator, or data from a drive or energy meter, gives a much better load estimate.

    Common mistakes

    • Using nameplate power for motors that run part-loaded.
    • Ignoring demand and reactive-energy charges.
    • Mixing kW (power) and kWh (energy).

    Where it’s used

    • Budgeting energy for equipment.
    • Comparing operating scenarios (hours, load).
    • First-pass screening of energy projects.

    Questions

    How do I convert kW to kWh?

    Multiply power in kW by operating time in hours. A 10 kW load running 5 hours uses 50 kWh.

    Why include motor efficiency?

    Motor nameplates give mechanical output. Electrical input is higher by the losses, so input = output ÷ efficiency.
