Select the nearest standard bank size above the result, and check harmonics (VFDs) before installing capacitors — detuned banks may be needed.

Before buying capacitors

  • Harmonics: drives, rectifiers and welders create harmonics that can resonate with capacitors. Detuned (reactor-protected) banks are often needed. A harmonic survey is recommended.
  • Load variation: automatic stepped banks follow changing loads and avoid over-correction (leading power factor) at light load.
  • Location: correcting at large motors also reduces current in the feeders upstream. Central correction is cheaper but only relieves the supply transformer and tariff.

Why aim for 0.95 rather than 1.0?

Each step towards unity needs more kvar for less benefit, and a bank sized for unity at full load over-corrects at partial load. Most tariffs set penalty thresholds between 0.9 and 0.95. Check your own utility’s rules.

Worked interpretation

In the example, raising the power factor from 0.78 to 0.95 needs about 237 kvar. Apparent power falls from 641 kVA to 526 kVA, freeing about 115 kVA of transformer and cable capacity. A standard 250 kvar automatic bank in steps would typically be selected, with step sizes matched to how the load varies.

Frequently asked questions

Does power factor correction reduce kWh?

Only slightly, through lower I²R losses in cables and transformers downstream of the capacitors. Most of the savings come from avoided reactive-energy penalties and released capacity.
