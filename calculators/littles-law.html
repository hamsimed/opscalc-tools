Lead Time Calculator (Little’s Law)

Little’s Law links three flow measures: work in process, throughput and lead time. If you know how much is in the system and how fast it leaves, you know how long an item waits on average. Add value-added time to calculate process cycle efficiency, a standard lean measure of waiting.

Process cycle efficiency compares value-added time to total lead time. Low values point to waiting and queues, not slow operations.

Why it matters

Little’s Law holds for any stable system over a long enough period, whatever the arrival or service distribution. It means the fastest way to cut lead time is often to cut WIP, not to speed up machines. Releasing less work into the system shortens queues while throughput stays the same.

Low process cycle efficiency is normal in batch environments. It shows that most of the lead time is waiting. That waiting is where value-stream mapping and pull systems help.

Conditions for accurate use

  • Use long-run averages. The relationship holds on average, not item by item.
  • The system should be roughly stable: WIP neither growing nor shrinking over the period.
  • Measure WIP and throughput at the same process boundary.

Frequently asked questions

Can I solve for WIP or throughput instead?

Yes. Rearranged: WIP = Throughput × Lead time, and Throughput = WIP ÷ Lead time. This calculator solves for lead time, which is the most common use.
