    TEEP Calculator

    Total Effective Equipment Performance (TEEP) measures good output against all calendar time, not only planned time. It combines OEE with loading (the share of the calendar you schedule), so it shows how much hidden capacity exists before you buy new equipment.

    h

    h

    Formula

    Loading = Planned production time ÷ Calendar timeTEEP = OEE × Loading
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    OEEOverall equipment effectiveness over the planned time%
    Planned production timeHours scheduled to run in the periodh
    Calendar timeAll hours in the period (24 × days)h

    Worked example

    A packaging line runs two shifts on five days (120 scheduled hours per 168-hour week) and achieves 65% OEE.

    Inputs
    OEE65 %
    Planned production time120 h
    Calendar time168 h
    Results
    TEEP46.4%
    Utilization (loading)71.4%
    Unscheduled time48 h
    Fully productive time78 h

    TEEP shows how much of all calendar time produces good parts at full speed. The gap to OEE is capacity left unused by the schedule, not by the equipment.

    OEE or TEEP?

    OEE answers “how well do we use the time we planned?”. TEEP answers “how well do we use all the time that exists?”. A plant can have good OEE and low TEEP if it runs only one shift. That gap is the cheapest capacity available: adding a shift or reducing planned downtime usually costs less than a new machine.

    Using TEEP in capacity decisions

    Before approving an investment for more capacity, compare required output with output at a realistic TEEP. If the existing asset could meet demand with an extra shift and modest OEE improvement, the investment case should be weighed against those options. Use the OEE calculator to break down the losses inside planned time.

    Common mistakes

    • Using planned time as the TEEP denominator: that gives OEE, not TEEP.
    • Mixing periods: OEE measured over a month with calendar hours of a week.
    • Treating low TEEP as an equipment problem when the cause is the schedule.

    Where it’s used

    • Capacity studies before capital investment.
    • Comparing shift patterns (2 × 8 h, 3 × 8 h, continuous).
    • Bottleneck analysis on continuous-process lines.

    Questions

    Can TEEP be higher than OEE?

    No. TEEP equals OEE multiplied by loading, which is at most 100%. TEEP equals OEE only for equipment scheduled 24 hours a day, every day.
