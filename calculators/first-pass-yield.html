    First Pass Yield & Scrap Rate Calculator

    First pass yield is the share of units that go through a process right the first time, with no rework or scrap. Final yield counts reworked units as good, so it hides the cost of fixing them. This calculator shows both, with scrap and rework rates.

    Formula

    FPY = Units passing first time ÷ Units enteringScrap rate = Units scrapped ÷ Units enteringRework rate = (Units entering − Passed first time − Scrapped) ÷ Units enteringFinal yield = (Units entering − Scrapped) ÷ Units entering
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    Units enteringUnits started in the process step during the periodunits
    Passed first timeUnits accepted without any rework or repairunits
    ScrappedUnits rejected and discardedunits

    Worked example

    A welding station received 2,000 frames. 1,820 passed inspection directly, 60 were scrapped, and the rest were repaired and passed.

    Inputs
    Units entering the process2,000 units
    Units passing first time (no rework)1,820 units
    Units scrapped60 units
    Results
    First pass yield91%
    Scrap rate3%
    Rework rate6%
    Final yield (after rework)97%
    Units reworked120 units

    Final yield hides the cost of rework; first pass yield exposes it. Track both.

    Rolled throughput yield

    For a multi-step process, multiply the FPY of each step to get rolled throughput yield (RTY), the probability that a unit passes every step first time. Three steps at 95% each give an RTY of about 85.7%. Small losses at each step add up quickly.

    Using FPY for improvement

    • Rank process steps by FPY loss (Pareto) to find where rework capacity goes.
    • Rework uses capacity twice. A low FPY at the bottleneck costs output directly.
    • Feed the quality factor of OEE with right-first-time units, consistent with FPY.

    Common mistakes

    • Counting reworked units as first-pass good.
    • Measuring at the end of the line only, which hides internal rework loops.
    • Mixing units started and units finished from different periods.

    Where it’s used

    • Quality KPIs per process step.
    • Prioritising rework reduction.
    • Feeding the quality factor of OEE.

    Questions

    Is FPY the same as first time right (FTR)?

    They are usually the same idea: the share of output produced correctly without rework. Some companies use FTR at order or document level and FPY at unit level.
