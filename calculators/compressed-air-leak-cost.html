    Compressed Air Leak Cost Calculator

    Leaks often consume a significant share of a compressor’s output, and the energy is paid every hour the system is pressurised. The load/unload test is a simple way to measure leakage: with all production demand off, the time the compressor spends loaded reflects only the leaks.

    Formula

    Leakage = Load time ÷ (Load time + Unload time)Energy lost = Compressor power × Leakage × Pressurised hoursCost = Energy lost × Energy price

    Worked example

    During a weekend shutdown a 75 kW compressor loads for 18 seconds and unloads for 42 seconds per cycle. The network stays pressurised 8,000 hours a year.

    Inputs
    Average load time per cycle18 s
    Average unload time per cycle42 s
    Compressor input power at full load75 kW
    Hours the system is pressurized per year8,000 h
    Energy price0.12 per kWh
    Results
    Annual cost of leaks21,600
    Leakage (share of compressor capacity)30%
    Annual energy lost to leaks180,000 kWh

    Load/unload test: run with all production demand off. Unloaded running power is ignored here, so the estimate is conservative for load/unload compressors.

    Running the test

    1. Stop all production air use (shutdown, weekend), but keep the network pressurised.
    2. Time at least five load/unload cycles and average them.
    3. For variable-speed compressors, record average power instead and compare with full-load power.

    A sequence of tags, repairs and re-tests turns the result into a tracked improvement project.

    Common mistakes

    • Testing with production still drawing air.
    • Timing a single cycle instead of an average.
    • Ignoring unloaded running power, which is a separate waste in load/unload compressors.

    Where it’s used

    • Leak-management programmes and energy audits.
    • Justifying ultrasonic leak detection.
    • Setting a baseline before and after repair campaigns.

    Questions

    What leakage level is acceptable?

    There is no universal figure. Track your own baseline and the trend after repair campaigns; any reduction is directly saved energy.
