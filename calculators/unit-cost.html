    Cost per Unit Calculator

    Cost per unit is the total production cost of a batch or period divided by the good units produced. Splitting it into materials, labor and overhead shows which cost drives the price and where improvement pays.

    Last reviewed 17 October 2026

    Formula

    Total cost = Materials + Direct labor + OverheadCost per unit = Total cost ÷ Good unitsMargin per unit = Price − Cost per unit
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    MaterialsRaw materials and components consumedcurrency
    Direct laborWages and charges of production staff for the batch or periodcurrency
    OverheadIndirect costs allocated: energy, maintenance, depreciation, supervisioncurrency
    Good unitsUnits meeting specificationunits

    Worked example

    A batch of 2,500 good units used 18,000 of materials, 9,600 of direct labor and 7,400 of allocated overhead. The selling price is 17.50.

    Inputs
    Materials cost (batch or period)18,000
    Direct labor cost9,600
    Overhead allocated7,400
    Good units produced2,500 units
    Selling price per unit (optional)17.5
    Results
    Cost per unit14
    Total cost35,000
    Materials per unit7.2
    Labor per unit3.84
    Overhead per unit2.96
    Margin per unit3.5
    Margin on price20%

    Divide by good units only. Scrap and rework raise the real cost of each good unit.

    Using the breakdown

    If materials dominate, yield and purchasing matter most; scrap is expensive. If labor dominates, cycle time and line balance matter (see the labor productivity calculator). If overhead dominates, volume and asset utilisation matter, because fixed costs spread over more units.

    Common mistakes

    • Dividing by total units instead of good units.
    • Allocating overhead on a basis unrelated to what drives it.
    • Using full cost per unit for short-term pricing decisions where only variable costs change (see break-even).

    Where it’s used

    • Product costing and pricing.
    • Make-or-buy comparisons.
    • Quantifying the cost of scrap and rework.

    Questions

    Should depreciation be included?

    For full product cost, yes, as part of overhead. For short-term decisions, use the contribution margin approach in the break-even calculator.
