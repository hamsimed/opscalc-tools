Use inventory position, not on-hand stock

Compare the reorder point with inventory position (on hand + already on order − backorders), not just what is on the shelf. Otherwise you may reorder again while a previous order is still in transit.

Setting the safety stock

Safety stock protects against demand and lead-time variation during the replenishment cycle. The safety stock calculator sizes it statistically from the service level you target.

Reorder point and order quantity work together

The reorder point decides when to order. The order quantity decides how much. A common setup is a continuous-review (s, Q) system: when the inventory position falls to the reorder point s, order a fixed quantity Q. Q is often set with the EOQ calculator and rounded to pack sizes.

In a periodic-review system, where you check stock every week, for example, protection must cover the review period plus the lead time. Use (review period + lead time) in place of lead time when calculating demand and safety stock.

Frequently asked questions

What if demand is seasonal?

Recalculate the reorder point when the demand rate changes, or use a forecast of demand over the coming lead time instead of a historical average.
