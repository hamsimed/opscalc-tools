    Pump Power Calculator

    Pump power follows directly from flow, head and fluid density. This calculator gives hydraulic power, the shaft power the motor must deliver, and the electrical power drawn from the supply, so you can size a motor or estimate energy cost at the duty point.

    Formula

    Hydraulic power (kW) = ρ × g × Q × H ÷ 1,000Shaft power = Hydraulic power ÷ Pump efficiencyElectrical input = Shaft power ÷ Motor efficiency

    TermMeaningUnit
    ρFluid densitykg/m³
    gGravitational acceleration, 9.80665m/s²
    QFlow rate (converted from m³/h to m³/s)m³/s

    Worked example

    A process water pump delivers 180 m³/h against 45 m of total head. The pump is 72% efficient at that point, the motor 94%, and it runs 6,000 hours a year.

    Inputs
    Flow rate180 m³/h
    Total dynamic head45 m
    Fluid density1,000 kg/m³
    Pump efficiency72 %
    Motor efficiency94 %
    Operating hours per year (optional)6,000 h
    Results
    Electrical input power32.6 kW
    Pump shaft power30.65 kW
    Hydraulic power22.06 kW
    Wire-to-water efficiency67.7%
    Annual energy use195,611 kWh

    Select a motor with margin above shaft power at the duty point, and check power at the end of the pump curve.

    Reading the result

    Wire-to-water efficiency (hydraulic power ÷ electrical input) summarises the whole pumping set. Values well below the pump’s best-efficiency figure suggest the pump runs away from its best efficiency point, often because of oversizing or throttling. The affinity laws calculator estimates what speed control could save.

    Total dynamic head

    Head is the sum of static lift, pressure difference and friction losses in pipes and fittings at the actual flow. Read it from the system curve or from measured suction and discharge pressures. Using the pump’s nameplate head instead of the operating head is the most common source of error.

    Common mistakes

    • Using nameplate head instead of the head at the operating flow.
    • Forgetting density for slurries or brines, which can raise power markedly.
    • Sizing the motor exactly to shaft power at the duty point without margin for the end of the curve.

    Where it’s used

    • Motor sizing for new pumping duties.
    • Energy audits of pumping systems.
    • Checking measured motor power against the expected duty point.

    Questions

    Why multiply by 9.80665?

    Hydraulic power is the energy needed to lift the fluid mass per second: density × g × flow × head. g is standard gravity in m/s².
