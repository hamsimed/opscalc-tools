    MTBF Calculator

    Mean time between failures (MTBF) is the average operating time between failures of repairable equipment. The calculator also gives the failure rate and, with a mission time, the probability of running that long without failure under a constant-failure-rate assumption.

    Formula

    MTBF = Total operating time ÷ Number of failuresFailure rate λ = 1 ÷ MTBFReliability R(t) = e^(−t ÷ MTBF)
    Variables and units
    TermMeaningUnit
    Total operating timeRunning time of the equipment in the period, excluding repair and idle timeh
    Number of failuresFailures needing corrective action that stopped or degraded the functioncount
    Mission time tDuration for which you want the probability of no failureh

    Reliability assumes a constant failure rate (exponential model). It does not hold for wear-out failures.

    Getting a trustworthy MTBF

    • Define "failure". Agree which events count (functional failures only, or every corrective work order) and apply it consistently.
    • Use operating time, not calendar time, for continuously wearing equipment. For standby equipment, use calendar time and say so.
    • Watch sample size. With few failures, MTBF is uncertain. A single extra failure can change it a lot.

    Limits of the reliability estimate

    The exponential formula assumes failures happen at a constant rate: random, with no wear-out. Bearings, belts, liners and other wear components usually have rising failure rates. For those, a Weibull analysis of time-to-failure data is more realistic, and preventive replacement intervals should come from it.

    Standards

    IEC 60050-192 (International Electrotechnical Vocabulary, dependability) and EN 13306 (maintenance terminology) give formal definitions of failure, MTBF and related terms. They are useful reference points when aligning maintenance KPIs between sites.

    Common mistakes

    • Using calendar time when operating time is meant.
    • Counting minor stops or preventive work as failures.
    • Reading MTBF as the expected life of one component.

    Where it’s used

    • Reliability KPIs for critical equipment.
    • Spare parts and maintenance planning.
    • Comparing equipment generations or suppliers.

    Questions

    What is the difference between MTBF and MTTF?

    MTBF applies to repairable items that return to service after a failure. MTTF (mean time to failure) applies to non-repairable items that are replaced.

    Does MTBF include repair time?

    Not in the operating-time definition used here. Some organisations use calendar time between failures. That figure includes repair time and should be labelled differently.
