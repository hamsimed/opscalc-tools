Downtime Cost Calculator

A stop costs more than its repair bill. This calculator adds up lost contribution margin, idle labour and other direct costs to give the total cost of a downtime event and a cost per hour. Use the cost per hour to rank improvement projects and justify spares.

Costs are in your own currency. Lost margin applies only if the output is truly lost (bottleneck stopped, no recovery capacity).

When is output really lost?

Lost margin applies only if the output cannot be recovered. A stop at a non-bottleneck with buffers or spare capacity may cost almost nothing in sales. A stop at the bottleneck of a sold-out plant loses margin for every hour. Use 0 for margin when the volume will be recovered without overtime, and count the overtime cost instead.

Using cost per hour

  • Multiply by annual downtime hours per asset to rank where reliability work pays most.
  • Compare with the cost of holding a critical spare, or of a redundancy investment.
  • Feed the result into the payback calculator for improvement projects.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use selling price instead of margin?

Not usually. When output is lost, you also avoid its variable costs (materials, energy). Contribution margin is the money actually lost.
