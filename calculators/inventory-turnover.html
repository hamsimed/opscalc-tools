Inventory Turnover Calculator

Inventory turnover shows how many times inventory is used up and replaced in a period. Days inventory outstanding (DIO) gives the same information as the average number of days stock sits before it is used or sold.

Interpreting the ratio

Higher turnover means less capital tied up per unit of sales. But very high turnover can also signal stockouts and lost sales. Benchmarks vary widely by industry, so compare with your own history and with peers in the same sector.

Two data points (start and end) can misrepresent seasonal businesses. Use monthly averages where possible.

Turnover by item class

A single turnover figure for the whole warehouse averages fast movers with slow and obsolete stock. Calculate turnover by ABC class or product family. Then list the items with no movement in the last 6 or 12 months. They tie up capital and space and usually need a decision: use, sell, or write off.

For maintenance spares, low turnover is often intentional. Critical insurance spares are held for availability, not rotation. Judge them by the downtime risk they cover (see the downtime cost calculator), not by turnover.

Frequently asked questions

Why use COGS instead of sales?

Inventory is valued at cost, so COGS is the consistent numerator. Using sales (at selling price) inflates the ratio.
