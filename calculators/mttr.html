Break MTTR down to reduce it

Total MTTR hides very different activities. Splitting it into response (time to get a technician on site), diagnosis, logistics (parts, tools, permits) and hands-on repair usually shows that waiting dominates. Waiting is cut through spares strategy, standard repair procedures and clear escalation rules, not faster wrenches.

Consistency checks

  • Use the same start and stop events for every work order: stop to restart is the most common choice.
  • Keep preventive maintenance separate: MTTR covers corrective work.
  • Report the median as well as the mean. A few long repairs can distort the average.

Typical MTTR improvement levers

  • Fault diagnosis: alarm lists with probable causes, troubleshooting guides for repeat failures, and condition data available at the machine.
  • Spares: critical spares stocked, identified and kitted. A part that exists but cannot be found adds to repair time.
  • Standard work for repairs: documented procedures for frequent repairs, with tools and isolation points listed.
  • Modular replacement: replace an assembly and repair it off-line, instead of repairing in place.

Measure each lever’s effect by tracking the components of MTTR (response, diagnosis, logistics, repair) separately for a few months.

Frequently asked questions

What does the "R" in MTTR stand for?

Usually "repair", but some organisations use "restore", "recovery" or "respond". Write down which one you mean, because the time boundaries differ.
